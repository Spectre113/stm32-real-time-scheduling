\subsection{Super Loop Scheduler}

Let us first consider an example of a Super Loop schedule for two periodic tasks with implicit deadlines ($D_i = T_i$).

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Task set used in the Super Loop example.}
  \label{tab:superloop_tasks}
  \begin{tabular}{c|ccc}
    \hline
    \textbf{Task} & $C_i$ & $T_i$ & $D_i$ \\
    \hline
    $\tau_1$ & 20 & 50 & 50 \\
    $\tau_2$ & 10 & 20 & 20 \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Here, $C_i$, $T_i$, and $D_i$ denote execution time, period and relative deadline respectively.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
   \hspace*{-0.9cm}
  \includegraphics[width=1.27\columnwidth]{SuperLoop ex.png}
  \caption{Super Loop execution scenario for the task set in Table~\ref{tab:superloop_tasks}.}
  \label{fig:superloop_schedule}
\end{figure}

In the considered Super Loop implementation, ready tasks execute in a fixed order and each selected job runs to completion.

The corresponding scheduling state is shown in Table~\ref{tab:superloop_state}.

Let $R_i^k$ denote the scheduled release time of job $J_i^k$ and $t_i^{next}$ the next release time tracked for task $\tau_i$. At job completion, $t^{cur}$ denotes current system time, a deadline miss is recorded if $t^{cur} > R_i^k + D_i$. If $R_i^j < t^{cur}$, the given release time is skipped and $t_i^{next}$ is incremented again. A release scheduled for $t_i^{next} = t^{cur}$ is retained.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:superloop_schedule}, $J_1^1$ executes from $t=0$ to $t=20$. Then, $J_2^1$ executes until $t=30$, missing its deadline at $t=20$. At completion, the release scheduled for $R_2^2=20$ is counted as skipped and $t_2^{next}$ advances to $R_2^3=40$. Thus, no $J_2^2$ is executed.

\section{Background on EDF Real-Time Scheduling}
The Super Loop mechanism does not take deadlines into account, jobs are selected according to a fixed loop order rather than their deadlines. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:superloop_schedule}, this may delay jobs beyond their deadlines. The considered implementation skips releases according to the rule above.

We consider preemptive EDF, which dynamically selects the ready job with the earliest absolute deadline. For a job $J_i^k$ released at $R_i^k$, its absolute deadline is

\[d_i^k = R_i^k + D_i\]

If a newly released job has an earlier deadline than the running job, the running job is preempted.
