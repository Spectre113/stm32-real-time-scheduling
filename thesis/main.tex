\subsection{Super Loop Scheduler}

Let us first consider an example of a Super Loop schedule for two
periodic tasks with implicit deadlines ($D_i = T_i$).

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Task set used in the Super Loop example.}
  \label{tab:superloop_tasks}
  \begin{tabular}{c|ccc}
    \hline
    \textbf{Task} & $C_i$ & $T_i$ & $D_i$ \\
    \hline
    $\tau_1$ & 20 & 50 & 50 \\
    $\tau_2$ & 10 & 20 & 20 \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Here, $C_i$, $T_i$, and $D_i$ denote execution time, period, and relative deadline respectively.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \hspace*{-0.08\columnwidth}
  \includegraphics[width=1.25\columnwidth]{SuperLoop ex.png}
  \caption{Super Loop execution scenario for the task set in Table~\ref{tab:superloop_tasks}.}
  \label{fig:superloop_schedule}
\end{figure}

Super Loop checks tasks sequentially in a fixed order, while each selected job executes to completion before the next task is checked.

The corresponding scheduling state is shown in Table~\ref{tab:superloop_state}.

A deadline miss is detected when $t^{cur} > R_i^k + D_i$, while a release is skipped when
$t^{cur} > t_i^{next}$.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering

In Fig.~\ref{fig:superloop_schedule}, $J_1^1$ executes from $t=0$ to $t=20$, delaying $J_2^1$ beyond its deadline. At $t=20$, $\tau_2$ reaches its next
scheduled release time while $J_2^1$ is still pending; therefore, this release is skipped and no $J_2^2$ is created.

\section{Background on EDF Real-Time Scheduling}
Super Loop is deadline-unaware: jobs are selected according to a fixed loop order
rather than their deadlines. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:superloop_schedule}, this may delay jobs beyond their deadlines and cause skipped releases.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Super Loop and EDF scheduling principles.}
  \label{tab:sl_edf_comparison}

  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \hline
    \textbf{Property} & \textbf{Super Loop} & \textbf{EDF} \\
    \hline
    Job selection & Fixed loop order & Earliest deadline \\
    Deadline-aware & No & Yes \\
    Preemption & No & Yes \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

EDF dynamically selects the ready job with the earliest absolute deadline.
For a job $J_i^k$ released at $R_i^k$, its absolute deadline is

\[d_i^k = R_i^k + D_i\]

If a newly released job has an earlier deadline than the running job,
the running job is preempted.

For the same task set as in Section II-B, the resulting EDF schedule is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:edf_schedule}. Observe that, unlike Super Loop, EDF:
\begin{itemize}
  \item selects the ready job with the earliest absolute deadline;
  \item preempts the running job when a job with an earlier deadline is released;
  \item avoids the deadline miss observed under Super Loop for the considered task set.
\end{itemize}
